Forecast methods

Ridge baseline

Fits recent concentration, its change, neighboring gradients and temperature. Trained on generated fields.

Persistence

Holds the latest concentration fixed. A transparent comparison baseline for short-range forecasts.

Imported frames

Uses your supplied 6-hour concentration forecasts directly. No synthetic model is applied to those frames.

Scenario ice evolution

This is the current scenario, not the held-out benchmark.

Iceberg method

Geometry-dependent wind contribution is combined with spatially varying ocean currents. Forty ensemble members are integrated with midpoint steps of 30 minutes.

Reduced equilibrium drag; static forcing. Coriolis, sea-ice mechanics, melting and iceberg grounding are not represented. Compare with a validated physics model before operational use.

Import environmental data

JSON preserves scenarios, iceberg records and optional forecasts. CSV accepts a complete longitude/latitude grid and resamples it to 30 × 44 cells.

Input fields & units

CSV columns: longitude, latitude, current, previous, temperature, land, wind_u, wind_v, current_u, current_v. Optional: wave_height, depth, observed_at. Concentration is a 0–1 fraction; temperature °C; wind/current m/s, east/north positive; wave/depth metres. Previous concentration is six hours earlier.

JSON grids are 30 × 44, north row first. Southern Ocean regions 45–85°S, up to 30° wide and 15° high. No dateline crossing. Missing values are rejected. Optional forecast_frames includes time zero and each 6-hour lead.

The science behind the plan

Build on evidence.

Antarctic forecasting, iceberg physics and the gaps we still need to close.

Start with SIPNet for Antarctic forecasting.

Use the iceberg-drift paper to understand the physics. Keep the published model results separate from this prototype’s synthetic benchmark.

What would make this field-ready?

  1. Prepare Antarctic observation sequences with time-separated training, validation and test years.
  2. Benchmark against persistence and climatology, with separate ice-edge and concentration metrics.
  3. Validate iceberg tracks against observed positions and calibrate the uncertainty.
  4. Use verified bathymetry, forecast forcing and vessel-specific speed/resistance data for routing.

These are research tasks, not completed integrations. A paper’s published performance cannot be transferred to this app.
